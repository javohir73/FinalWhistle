FinalWhistle · Multi-sport expansion · Design concepts

Three UX concepts for one platform:
World Cup → EPL · LaLiga · Bundesliga · NRL

What I read in the codebase
  • NRL already lives at /nrl/* with its own ladder, tips, round pages — a parallel tree, not a shared system.
  • Reusable as-is: ProbabilityBar, ChanceChip, ConfidenceBadge, Flag/ClubBadge, glass Card, FormStrip, Sparkline, ShareButton.
  • Should generalise: MatchCard vs SportMatchCard (near-twins → one SportMatchCard), GroupTable vs LadderTable (→ one StandingsTable with zone config), SiteNav/BottomNav link maps.
  • Scaling risks: per-sport routes hard-code terminology; two leaderboards; SportSwitcher is binary (Football/NRL) — breaks at 5+ competitions; no "my competitions" memory.
  • A11y: probability communicated by colour alone in bars (needs labels); 10px nav type below WCAG comfort; lime-on-dark passes, amber-on-dark is borderline for small text.
Market research → how we stand out
  • FotMob wins on calm simplicity + a followed-teams home. Sofascore wins on depth but users call it congested and ad-heavy.
  • Top complaints across all rivals: ads over live moments, notification spam, UI churn. FinalWhistle's edge: no ads, explainable AI, play-against-the-model.
  • Nobody pairs predictions with a public accuracy record — our "honest model" framing is unique shelf space.
  • Borrowed ideas: followed-first home (FotMob), momentum visuals (FotMob xG timeline → our win-prob timeline), fantasy-style play loop (Sofascore) recast as ad-free tipping vs the AI.
Recommendation
Ship 1b Clubhouse as the app's information architecture — its followed-first home and single competition switcher scale to any number of leagues without new nav, and it gives tipping and AI predictions the equal billing you asked for. Then borrow 1c Floodlight's match-centre treatment for match detail (that's the screen people screenshot), and offer 1a Terminal's dense rows as a "compact mode" for stats-heads. Shared nav decision (all three): two sport spaces at the top level — Football (with EPL, LaLiga, Bundesliga, WC26 as subsections) and NRL — never mixed in one feed; below that, one constant bottom bar (Home · Matches · Play · Tables · You) whose labels adapt per sport (Fixtures/Matches, Standings/Ladder).

1aTERMINAL — the data desk. Density, tables, one glance = whole matchday. For the serious user.
Direction — pitch-side data desk. Rows over cards, tabular numerals everywhere, zero decoration. Chrome recedes; numbers lead. Lime only for signal (win, live, active).
Navigation — mobile: sport segment (Football | NRL) with a league strip below it + 5-tab bar. Desktop: persistent left rail (sport → competition tree, pinnable). You always see which competition you're in — the strip never leaves.
Strengths / limits — fastest scan, scales to 20 leagues, loved by stats-heads. Risk: colder first impression for casual fans; hero moments are quieter.

1bCLUBHOUSE — followed-first. Your teams, your competitions, your round of picks. For everyone. ← recommended
Direction — followed-first. Home is built from what you follow (teams + competitions), not from a league you must pick. Glass cards, warm and tactile; the tipping loop and AI predictions share top billing. Closest to today's app — an evolution, not a rebuild.
Navigation — 5 constant tabs: Home · Matches · Play · Tables · You. One "Competitions" screen (from the chip row or Tables) replaces the binary sport switcher; following a competition adds it everywhere; football and NRL feeds stay separate sections, never interleaved. Labels adapt: Fixtures/Standings for football, Matches/Ladder for NRL.
Strengths / limits — instantly personal, scales to N sports with zero new nav, best onboarding story ("pick your teams"). Risk: needs a good empty state before the user follows anything; card feed is less scannable than Terminal's rows.

1cFLOODLIGHT — cinematic broadcast. Full-bleed, giant type, the match as theatre. For brand distinctiveness.
Direction — the match as broadcast theatre. Full-bleed surfaces, oversized Bricolage numerals, TV scorebug language, lime used as floodlight glow. Minimal chrome; screens pace vertically like a story.
Navigation — same 5 constant tabs; the competition switcher is a full-screen typographic overlay (tap the competition name anywhere). Matches hang off a kickoff timeline spine. Desktop is a theatre: full-width hero + horizontal rails.
Strengths / limits — unmistakable identity, the most shareable match screens, best live experience. Risk: lower information density; big type needs discipline on small phones; heaviest build effort.
